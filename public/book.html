Monsters Ink LLC · a record

The Book

A year of sending, and the answers that came back. From the first receipt in February to the last letter in September.

Taylor Heller. Built only from letters, receipts, and screenshots. A sentence in a letter is not a grant.

Preface

This book is the record of one inventor writing to companies and to the government, and of what those offices wrote back. It is not a theory. It is not a claim that the work was accepted. It is the sequence, in the order the paper moved.

Four kinds of sentence appear. A closed fact is something a retrieved letter, receipt, or screenshot shows. A statement is something Taylor said, with no matching receipt in this pass. An open item has no closing document. A refused sentence was written, and the rest of the record does not support it.

The application at the center is 19/658,750. On the filing receipt its title is a magnetic-assisted progressive suspension with a 1.2 MHz resonant frequency lock. In the March messages the same work is called ARES-UCX. Those are two names in two documents. This book does not weld them into a third thing. MAGPIE, where that word is used, means application 19/658,750.

Street addresses, phone numbers, and card numbers are left out. The application numbers stay, because they are printed on the receipts.

Audit. If a scene is not in a retrieved frame, it is not in this book.

1. The filing

Before any of the letters, there is a payment.

On 27 April 2026, at 04:25 in the morning Eastern, a basic utility filing fee was paid on application 19/658,750. The receipt is an electronic payment receipt from the Patent Office. Confirmation 3591. Patent Center number 75547675. The person who filed it is Kathryn Nuzum. The first named inventor is Taylor Heller. The type line says utility, nonprovisional, under 35 USC 111(a).

The title on that receipt is long, and it is specific. Magnetic-assisted progressive suspension. A 1.2 MHz resonant frequency lock. A zero-point equilibrium architecture. The fee line is code 4011, seventy dollars. The patent-number line is blank.

A blank patent number is the whole point of the page. The Office took the fee and opened a file. It did not issue a patent. Later letters will forget that distinction. The receipt does not.

The same title appears again on a Patent Center row dated 9 May, center number 76049174. That row does not say what document was filed. It only says the application and the title. An information disclosure statement was described, later, as already sent. This book can say the 9 May row exists. It cannot say the row is that statement.

Audit. Closed: the payment, the title, the confirmation, the filer, the blank patent number. Refused: any reading of this page as a grant.

2. Oshkosh

The first outside conversation in the frames is not a letter. It is a text, on 18 March 2026.

At 3:17 in the afternoon Taylor wrote to a Mr. Schulte. The subject, in ordinary words, was a patent-pending suspension for heavy vehicles: stability, load, and how long the parts last. The message said it was not a sales pitch. Schulte did not answer the engineering. He asked who had already been reached.

The answer was a small story of a missed connection. A call through the website. An operator. A transfer to IT. A dropped call. A woman named Dawn. Then an explanation that three provisional applications had been filed, and that an earlier message had been drafted with ChatGPT, and that it might have looked like a robot. Schulte wrote Copy, said he would get back in a few days, and then did the useful thing. He named Erik Ellifson. The thread carries a read stamp for that day.

At 4:44 the same afternoon Taylor wrote to Ellifson and named the referral. Ellifson agreed to a short conversation. He offered the next day, or the Thursday after PTO, and two times, 11:00 and 1:00 Central. Taylor took 1:00 and asked for a phone call rather than Teams. That exchange is the scheduling. It is not the call.

The next day, 19 March, at 1:04, seven photos went across. The frames show a figure labeled FIG. 12, Vortex / Flow Integration, with flow arrows. They show a drawing of a hybrid magnetic-hydraulic cartridge. They show a photograph of truck suspension hardware. The bundle is marked read the same day.

Then the frames stop. No price. No review line. No refusal. The read stamp proves the pictures arrived. It does not prove they were discussed.

Audit. Closed: the referral, the time that was set, the seven photos, the read stamps. Open: whether the call happened, and anything after the pictures.

3. The notice

On the morning of 4 May 2026, the same morning the Federal Register published an Air Force notice, a letter went out from monstersink.business@gmail.com. It was 7:06. It was addressed to Karleine M. Justice at the Air Force Institute of Technology, and to John DePinto. A PDF of the notice was attached.

The letter called itself a prior-art notice on docket AFIT PLA 2026-01, tied to application 19/658,750. Then it said a thing the receipt does not say. It said that as of that morning the Patent Office had finalized a grant on 19/658,750. That sentence is in the letter. It is not in any receipt in this book. Justice's answer, the next day, treats the application as not yet granted. The sentence is refused.

Justice wrote back the same day, and she corrected the subject. The docket was an intent to grant a partial exclusive license on a patent the Air Force already held: 11,856,012 B2, a passive physical-layer cyber monitor, issued 26 December 2023. She wrote that this patent was not the subject of the protest. She attached notice 2026-08589, Federal Register volume 91, number 85, 4 May 2026, page 23977. Objections were due fifteen days after publication. She also said DePinto's address on the notice looked wrong, and that a slice of an AFRL notice, 2026-08598, was printed with it.

Taylor thanked her, said the isolation in that notice was governed by the resonant-lock claims, and asked for a mutual nondisclosure agreement before 19 May, with a contact for infrastructure synchronization.

On 5 May Justice closed that door in one paragraph. From the serial number, the application appeared to have been filed after the 2023 patent issued. First-to-file, in her view, meant no effect on the license. The Air Force did not want confidential information and would not sign an NDA. If the patent later granted, it could be sent. Her signature block is a research grants engineer at AFIT, Wright-Patterson. The footer says the message was not sent as controlled unclassified information. She noted she would have limited email from 11 to 15 May.

The frames are Air Force. An Army exchange on 4 May was named later. It is not in the inbox that was searched, and it is not these frames.

Audit. Closed: the notice, the 2023 patent, the fifteen-day window, the refusal of an NDA. Refused: the grant sentence. The window closed in May.

4. The innovation unit

From June through September the Defense Innovation Unit wrote to yeti.monstersink@gmail.com. The letters are short. A receipt means the form arrived inside the window. A non-select means the solution does not meet current needs, and that no feedback will be given. Neither letter is a contract.

On 22 June, DIU said an All Players solution was in review. Pitches for a second phase could start as early as 7 July. A rejection would also be mailed. On 25 June the rejection came, for a submission titled Grimm Law: A Universal Triadic Architect.

July is a run of receipts, several on the same solicitations, with short titles. Farseer, quantum sensing, title T0LM, ingested 2 July. Massed Modular Aircraft, titles Submission and Solution, on 20 July, and SDM MMA on 23 July. Specular MIST, titles Monsters Ink and T0LM, ingested 20 July, and Solvency Governance Engine on 26 July. SWAP-USV, ingested 28 July. On the early forms the CAGE line is blank, and the website line is a Gemini address. That is what the forms say.

Two of those tracks closed. SWAP-USV was non-selected on 28 August. On 2 September three Specular MIST titles were non-selected together: Monsters Ink, Solvency Governance Engine, and T0LM.

September is the other shape. On 21 September two Cue-Share forms were ingested, and the receipts arrived just after midnight on the 22nd. One is a space threat intelligence synthesis engine. One is Deep Blue Observer, a prize challenge. Both carry CAGE 24VL0. Both say the team will write within thirty days only if it needs more information. That clock runs to about 22 October 2026. Silence after that line is not a win, and it is not yet a loss.

Audit. Closed against the company: All Players, SWAP-USV, and the three Specular MIST titles. Open: the two Cue-Share receipts. The thirty-day line is not a selection.

5. The local door

On 3 September Taylor wrote to vdoga at the Nebraska APEX office. The ask was practical. An in-person counseling appointment at Mammel Hall, so a local firm could be pointed at the right Offutt lane before anyone wrote to the 55th contracting squadron. Two things were requested: a plain read of a one-page capability statement, and a check of the SAM posture.

The letter was careful about the posture. It gave the UEI, R9SQEQR51E46. It said the IRS name match had passed on 22 July. It said Active status was not confirmed. It said the CAGE was pending, and so no code would be stated. No reply to that letter is in the frames.

A capability statement did move, on a different thread. On 9 September Harold Sargus, a consultant at Nebraska APEX, said he would look the next week. On 17 September he sent formatting comments. On 22 September a revision went back. On 25 September he said he would look the next week. On 28 September he said the revision was clearer, and he offered a Zoom.

Between the request and the revision, the registry moved. On 21 September at 19:53 UTC, SAM wrote that Monsters Ink LLC was Active. Same UEI. CAGE 24VL0. The 3 September letter was right not to state a code. The 21 September letter is the code.

Audit. Closed: the counseling request, the Sargus exchange, the activation. Open: a meeting with the contracting squadron. It is not in these frames.

7. The claims file

A local file named CPI 1.pdf is a claims text. It is not an office action, and it is not in the receipt table under that name.

The file describes a magnetic suspension locked near 1.2 MHz, an angle near 53.13 degrees, a scaling close at 48, coils meant to harvest from the oscillation, and a line that asks for a royalty of 2 percent of gross revenue. The abstract writes a short formula, one-half G minus one-ninety-sixth G squared, and says it closes at G = 48. The claims run from a system, to a method, to that royalty sentence.

This chapter can say the file contains those words. It cannot say an examiner accepted them, or that the angle was measured, or that anyone owes the royalty. No action on this file was in the inbox.

Audit. The file is a text the inventor wrote. It is not a grant, and it is not a license.

8. What did not close

Cue-Share is the only government pair still inside a stated window, to about 22 October 2026. The letters say they will write if they need more. They do not say they have chosen.

Ellifson has the pictures, and the pictures were read on 19 March. Nothing after that read stamp is in the frames.

An Army exchange on 4 May was named. The search of the yeti inbox did not find it. The frames from that morning are the Air Force thread.

An information disclosure statement was described as filed. The nearest receipt is the 9 May row, and that row does not name the paper.

A file called Grimm reaper was modified in a drive on 30 August. A modified date is not a delivery.

A license ask, a hundred million dollars, United States only, was drafted and not sent. It is not an offer.

Audit. Open is the absence of a closing document. It is not a prediction.

Colophon

This is the book of the record. Eight chapters. The filing, the truck company, the Air Force notice, the innovation unit, the local accelerator, the receipt page, the claims file, and the edges that did not close.

Nothing in it is an award. Nothing in it is a license. Nothing in it is a secrecy grant. Application 19/658,750 remains pending. The receipt of 27 April remains a seventy-dollar filing payment. The grant sentence of 4 May remains a sentence.

Set 4 October 2026, from the closed papers only. The notes behind this text stay in the ledger folder. No second site.
